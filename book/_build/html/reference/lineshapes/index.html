Lineshapes, edges and scaling

This reference summarises the supplied NEXAFSFit evaluator used by GUI 0.2.24 throughout this manual. The equations describe the implemented generic model, rather than a choice of alternative textbook edge models.

Energy and integrated area¶

The model evaluates profiles at effective energy

Eeff=E+dE.E_{\mathrm{eff}} = E + dE.Eeff​=E+dE.
(1)

A symmetric peak with native position mu is therefore plotted at mu - dE. Energy alignment during preparation changes the data energy axis; dE is a separate optional model parameter. Do not confuse the two. The reference-calibration definition describes the acquisition offset; GUI preparation shows how to apply it.

Each peak contribution is

Ip(E)=g sp Ap fp(Eeff),I_p(E) = g\,s_p\,A_p\,f_p(E_{\mathrm{eff}}),Ip​(E)=gsp​Ap​fp​(Eeff​),
(2)

where gain is the global factor g, scale_param optionally names a scale s, and the profile f has unit area over the full energy axis. Omitted gain and scale act as one. A is intrinsic integrated area; the plotted contribution has area g s A over the infinite domain. A finite scan/window generally captures less. The position/area drag controls convert a requested component height back into this integrated-area convention.

A group name only collects peak sums. Assigning a peak to “bulk” does not multiply its area, share its width or constrain its position.

Supported peak shapes¶

Shape IDAdjustable shape parametersWidth convention
gaussmu, sigmaGaussian standard deviation
lorentzmu; gamma is staticLorentzian half-width at half maximum

emgmu, sigma, tauGaussian mean/width with an exponential tail

Every shape also has integrated area A. Active parameters can be fixed or varied; “adjustable” does not mean every value should be allowed to move in every fit.

For a Gaussian,

fG(x)=exp⁡[−(x−μ)2/(2σ2)]σ2π.f_G(x)=\frac{\exp[-(x-\mu)^2/(2\sigma^2)]}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}.fG​(x)=σ2π​exp[−(x−μ)2/(2σ2)]​.
(3)

Its FWHM is approximately 2.355 sigma, and its height at unit gain/scale is A / (sigma * sqrt(2*pi)).

For a Lorentzian,

fL(x)=γπ[(x−μ)2+γ2].f_L(x)=\frac{\gamma}{\pi[(x-\mu)^2+\gamma^2]}.fL​(x)=π[(x−μ)2+γ2]γ​.
(4)

Its FWHM is 2 gamma. The engine evaluates the unit-area Voigt with the Faddeeva function. Voigt sigma and gamma describe the Gaussian and Lorentzian constituents; neither is the total Voigt FWHM.

EMG interpretation¶

The engine uses its stable Delley EMG implementation. mu is the underlying Gaussian mean. Positive tau produces the higher-energy tail used by this GUI, and the apparent maximum generally differs from mu. Tau approaching zero gives the Gaussian limit. The editor restricts tau to nonnegative values; it does not offer a separate negative-tau tail interpretation.

Top-level gamma is a static specification for Lorentzian/Voigt. It is unused by EMG, even when retained in an older model. Do not interpret an unused gamma as an extra EMG broadening. For the corresponding numerical and graphical controls, see model parameter states and EMG asymmetry dragging.

Background and ionisation edges¶

The linear background lies outside global gain:

B(E)=b0+b1(Eeff−Eeff‾).B(E)=b_0+b_1\left(E_{\mathrm{eff}}-\overline{E_{\mathrm{eff}}}\right).B(E)=b0​+b1​(Eeff​−Eeff​​).
(5)

The mean is taken over the full grid supplied to evaluation. The GUI evaluates on the complete spectrum before applying a fit mask, keeping that reference stable when the fitting range is narrowed. Cropping arrays before calling a native evaluator instead changes this reference.

The generic edge uses the smooth step

S(E)=12[1+erf⁡(Eeff−Eedge2 wedge)],S(E)=\frac12\left[1+\operatorname{erf} \left(\frac{E_{\mathrm{eff}}-E_{\mathrm{edge}}}{\sqrt{2}\,w_{\mathrm{edge}}}\right)\right],S(E)=21​[1+erf(2​wedge​Eeff​−Eedge​​)],
(6)

with contribution

Iedge(E)=g [h+m(Eeff−Eedge)] S(E).I_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)=g\,[h+m(E_{\mathrm{eff}}-E_{\mathrm{edge}})]\,S(E).Iedge​(E)=g[h+m(Eeff​−Eedge​)]S(E).
(7)

Here h is edge_step, m is post_slope and w is w_edge. Multiple GUI edges add contributions with this same convention. The total is background plus edges plus peaks. Background, gain and model shift are optional in new GUI models.

Avoid adding redundant gain/scale/area freedoms unless you have a way to identify them: several multiplicative parameters can trade off without establishing a unique physical area. Use the model editor to add those terms, edge-to-peak links to relate positions, or edge subtraction to inspect their visual contribution without changing the fitting arrays.

Reference energy calibration¶

Native calibrate_energy_with_reference_foil locates a chosen maximum/minimum of reference/I0 within a search window on the original measured energy axis. argextreme selects the measured extreme. parab3 refines it with the vertex of the quadratic through that sample and its two immediate in-window neighbours in acquisition order. At a window boundary it falls back to the measured point; degenerate or linear three-point inputs also use the centre point. This is local interpolation, not an independently optimised peak-shape fit.

The data offset is target_energy - feature_energy, applied equally to all parallel acquisition channels. A separate photodiode acquisition is calibrated independently. No baseline/scale windows are involved in finding this reference feature. The later baseline and scale windows use aligned energies. This data alignment offset is distinct from the model’s optional dE.

Choose reference windows and targets appropriate to the acquisition. The native function can construct reference/I0 when absent; its method default is argextreme, whereas the GUI initially selects parab3. The GUI’s optional inspection panel retains the native input trace and output feature/offset. Its local curve uses the same three-point formula without rerunning calibration or modifying the result. An out-of-window native vertex is reported without silently moving or clipping it.

Preparation scaling¶

For a baseline window WbW_bWb​, preparation first subtracts b=mean⁡Ei∈Wbyib=\operatorname{mean}_{E_i\in W_b} y_ib=meanEi​∈Wb​​yi​. Mean scaling uses D=mean⁡Ei∈Ws(yi−b)D=\operatorname{mean}_{E_i\in W_s}(y_i-b)D=meanEi​∈Ws​​(yi​−b) and returns (yi−b)/D(y_i-b)/D(yi​−b)/D. This is the unchanged native prepare_fitting_signal behaviour and the GUI default. The native function has no selectable area-method argument.

The GUI’s optional area scaling instead uses D=∑i(Ei+1−Ei)[(yi−b)+(yi+1−b)]/2D=\sum_i (E_{i+1}-E_i)[(y_i-b)+(y_{i+1}-b)]/2D=∑i​(Ei+1​−Ei​)[(yi​−b)+(yi+1​−b)]/2, over consecutive measured points inside WsW_sWs​. At least two distinct points and a finite nonzero denominator are required. The integration is signed, accounts for unequal energy spacing and adds no interpolated endpoints. It uses increasing measured energy; an acquisition with reversals first requires the user’s explicit sorting review.

This area denominator has signal-times-energy units. Setting the prepared window integral to one differs from setting its average height to one; neither quantity is an individual model peak area AAA. Baseline subtraction still uses a mean in both modes. Settings, native intermediates and actual scale denominators are retained without changing original data. See the GUI preparation procedure.
