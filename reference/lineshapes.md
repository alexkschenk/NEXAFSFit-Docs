# Lineshapes, edges and scaling

This reference summarises the supplied NEXAFSFit evaluator used by GUI **0.2.30**
throughout this manual. The equations describe the implemented generic
model, rather than a choice of alternative textbook edge models.

## Energy and integrated area

The model evaluates profiles at effective energy

```{math}
:label: equation-effective-energy
E_{\mathrm{eff}} = E + dE.
```

A symmetric peak with native position `mu` is therefore plotted at `mu - dE`.
Energy alignment during preparation changes the data energy axis; `dE` is a
separate optional model parameter. Do not confuse the two.
The [reference-calibration definition](preparation.md#reference-energy-calibration) describes
the acquisition offset; [GUI preparation](../gui/preparing-data.md) shows how to apply it.

### Peak area and multiplicative scales

Each peak contribution is

```{math}
:label: equation-peak-scaling
I_p(E) = g\,s_p\,A_p\,f_p(E_{\mathrm{eff}}),
```

where `gain` is the global factor g, `scale_param` optionally names a scale s,
and the profile f has unit area over the full energy axis. Omitted gain and scale
act as one.

:::{important} A is integrated area, not peak height
`A` is intrinsic integrated area; the plotted contribution has area g s A over
the infinite domain. A finite scan/window generally captures less. Changing a
peak's width can change its height without changing A.
:::

The [position/area drag controls](../gui/drag-controls.md#move-position-or-change-height)
convert a requested component height back into this integrated-area convention.

A group name only collects peak sums. Assigning a peak to “bulk” does not multiply
its area, share its width or constrain its position.

## Supported peak shapes

| Shape ID | Adjustable shape parameters | Width convention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `gauss` | `mu`, `sigma` | Gaussian standard deviation |
| `lorentz` | `mu`; `gamma` is static | Lorentzian half-width at half maximum |
| `voigt` | `mu`, `sigma`; `gamma` is static | Gaussian sigma convolved with Lorentzian HWHM |
| `emg` | `mu`, `sigma`, `tau` | Gaussian mean/width with an exponential tail |

Every shape also has integrated area A. Active parameters can be fixed or varied;
“adjustable” does not mean every value should be allowed to move in every fit.

### Gaussian profile

For a Gaussian,

```{math}
:label: equation-gaussian-profile
f_G(x)=\frac{\exp[-(x-\mu)^2/(2\sigma^2)]}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}.
```

Its FWHM is approximately 2.355 sigma, and its height at unit gain/scale is
`A / (sigma * sqrt(2*pi))`.

### Lorentzian and Voigt profiles

For a Lorentzian,

```{math}
:label: equation-lorentzian-profile
f_L(x)=\frac{\gamma}{\pi[(x-\mu)^2+\gamma^2]}.
```

Its FWHM is 2 gamma. The engine evaluates the unit-area Voigt with the Faddeeva
function. Voigt sigma and gamma describe the Gaussian and Lorentzian constituents;
neither is the total Voigt FWHM.

### EMG interpretation

The engine uses its stable Delley EMG implementation. `mu` is the underlying
Gaussian mean. Positive tau produces the higher-energy tail used by this GUI,
and the apparent maximum generally differs from mu. Tau approaching zero gives
the Gaussian limit. The editor restricts tau to nonnegative values; it does not
offer a separate negative-tau tail interpretation.

:::{note} Gamma depends on the selected lineshape
Top-level gamma is a static specification for Lorentzian/Voigt. It is unused by
EMG, even when retained in an older model. Do not interpret an unused gamma as an
extra EMG broadening.
:::

For the corresponding numerical and graphical controls, see
[model parameter states](../gui/parameter-table.md#values-bounds-free-and-sharing)
and [EMG asymmetry dragging](../gui/drag-controls.md#change-emg-asymmetry).

## Background and ionisation edges

### Linear background and its reference grid

The linear background lies **outside** global gain:

```{math}
:label: equation-linear-background
B(E)=b_0+b_1\left(E_{\mathrm{eff}}-\overline{E_{\mathrm{eff}}}\right).
```

The mean is taken over the full grid supplied to evaluation. The GUI evaluates
on the complete spectrum before applying a fit mask, keeping that reference
stable when the fitting range is narrowed. Cropping arrays before calling a
native evaluator instead changes this reference.

### Ionisation-edge contribution

The generic edge uses the smooth step

```{math}
:label: equation-edge-step
S(E)=\frac12\left[1+\operatorname{erf}
\left(\frac{E_{\mathrm{eff}}-E_{\mathrm{edge}}}{\sqrt{2}\,w_{\mathrm{edge}}}\right)\right],
```

with contribution

```{math}
:label: equation-ionisation-edge
I_{\mathrm{edge}}(E)=g\,[h+m(E_{\mathrm{eff}}-E_{\mathrm{edge}})]\,S(E).
```

Here h is `edge_step`, m is `post_slope` and w is `w_edge`. Multiple GUI edges
add contributions with this same convention. The total is background plus edges
plus peaks. Background, gain and model shift are optional in new GUI models.

:::{warning} Multiplicative freedoms can be degenerate
Avoid adding redundant gain/scale/area freedoms unless you have a way to identify
them: several multiplicative parameters can trade off without establishing a
unique physical area.
:::

Use the [model editor](../gui/model-editor.md#add-edges-and-global-corrections-explicitly)
to add those terms, [edge-to-peak links](../gui/edge-links.md#edge-links-position)
to relate positions, or [edge subtraction](../gui/edge-subtraction.md#subtract-current-edges)
to inspect their visual contribution without changing the fitting arrays.
